# Sangre y Jade — Version 0 art codex

## Visual thesis

The world is built from six anchors: warm jungle green, terracotta orange, luminous jade, obsidian black, quetzal teal, and cacao brown. v0.2 uses detailed pixel-art raster illustrations and compact chibi combat sprites, with violet enemies separated from warm heroes and terrain.

## The champions

- **Balam** is a broad triangle: jaguar mantle, low stance, and heavy black-edged macuahuitl. Gold and spotted brown communicate protection and impact.
- **Ixchel's Voice** is a vertical circle-and-staff silhouette. Pale cloth, jade beads, turquoise smoke, and a luminous scepter separate her from the crowd.
- **Kukul** is a moving diagonal: narrow body, fan of teal/red feathers, and the long atlatl line. His silhouette always suggests forward momentum.

## The worlds

- **The Overgrown Temple** uses clear greens, warm stone, and daylight for the most readable introductory arena.
- **The Temple Under a Blood Moon** keeps the same geometry but compresses it into wine-red and violet values.
- **The Sunken Cenote** shifts to blue-black stone and bioluminescent teal, signaling the most dangerous threshold.

## UI language

Gold borders reference worked ornaments; amber, jade, and deep cacao panels keep text readable. Square illustrated combat buttons, beveled rectangular menus, and a bundled Pixelify Sans font unify the pixel-art interface. Decorative markings are fantasy patterns, not historical transcriptions.

## Cultural framing

The project draws broad inspiration from Maya city-states, astronomy, cacao exchange, architecture, materials, and underworld traditions. Its city, characters, chronology, magic, and crisis are fictional. Decorative marks are abstract patterns rather than attempts to reproduce readable historical writing.
